# Doctor's view: a 4 to 5 minute walkthrough

**The idea in one line:** before the patient sits down, the doctor already knows who she is, what happened last time, what changed since, and what she is worried about. No digging through files.

*Speaking time is about 4 to 5 minutes. Each section below is about 30 to 40 seconds.*

---

## 1. Today's schedule (the first screen)

**What the doctor sees:** a calendar for the day, in 20-minute slots. Finished visits are marked *Completed* or *Did not attend*. A red "Now" line shows where we are. Free slots show only the time.

**Each appointment shows:** name, age, sex, why the patient came (in four or five words), and a colour for how much attention she needs: **red** (high), **amber** (more than usual), **green** (lower concern).

**Why it helps:** the doctor sees the whole day in one look and knows who needs more time. Click the next patient and her record opens.

---

## 2. The patient's page (Overview)

**Top bar:** her photo, name, age, and sex. One look and you know who you are talking to.

**The four panels under it:**
- **Conditions:** what she is being treated for.
- **Medicines:** each medicine, and how regularly she actually takes it.
- **Goals:** the targets the clinic set, next to where she is today.
- **Allergy:** shown clearly so it is never missed.

**Why it helps:** the safety basics are visible immediately. The goals and the medicine-taking sit side by side, so the first conversation can start from facts.

---

## 3. Visit brief (on the left)

Four short boxes: **Profile, Last visit, Since then, Concern today.** Hover on one and it opens to the side with the details.

- **Last visit:** what she came with, and what we decided.
- **Since then:** what happened in between, such as glucose, sleep, walks, medicines, and life events.
- **Concern today:** what she is worried about right now.

**Why it helps:** it replaces the minute a doctor spends remembering "where did we leave this?". The gap between visits is usually where the useful information is.

---

## 4. Domains (the eight health areas)

Body measures, glucose, blood pressure, cholesterol, kidney, liver, activity, and lifestyle. Each is a card with a colour dot (red, amber, green or grey) and the three numbers that matter most.

**Click a card** and a window opens:
- **Left:** the values. **Right:** a large graph over the years, with her goals and the colour bands drawn on it.
- **Events and episodes:** the story between two visits, such as symptoms, life events and what was decided. It shows what was happening when a number moved.

**Why it helps:** the doctor sees the trend and the reason, not just a number. Nothing is hidden behind a long page.

---

## 5. Predictions

**What it shows:** "if things carry on like this, where will this number be in a few months?" Each card gives today's value, the expected value, and a range.

**Click one** and you see a graph with three possible paths: carry on as now, go back to her best, or keep slipping. A short note explains why that is good or bad.

**Why it helps:** it moves the conversation from "what is it today" to "where is it heading, and is it worth acting now?" It uses only her own readings, and it is simple on purpose.

---

## 6. Interventions and recommendations

**What it shows:** a card for each situation, marked **Needs action, Keep an eye,** or **On track**, with a word such as *Improve, Reduce, Review,* or *Continue*. Opening a card shows what is happening and a few things the doctor could consider.

**Why it helps:** it is a prompt, not an order. The doctor decides. It helps nothing get forgotten during a busy day.

---

## 7. Visit notes (the glowing orb, top right)

A small living orb listens during the consultation. Click it and a notepad opens. The orb turns **pink when the doctor speaks and violet when the patient speaks**, and the conversation is written down as it happens.

**Why it helps:** the doctor looks at the patient, not at a keyboard. Notes are ready at the end of the visit. *(In this demo the conversation is scripted. A real version needs the patient's consent.)*

---

## 8. Where the PCU fits (the part behind the screen)

**PCU** is the patient's personal data store. Think of it as one organised folder that quietly collects, from many places:

- Wearable data: heart rate, steps, sleep.
- Glucose sensor readings.
- Lab reports and clinic visits.
- Meals, medicines taken, and a short daily diary.

Behind the scenes the PCU cleans this data, keeps her usual levels, and notices what changed. The doctor's view is simply the **front desk** of that: it turns months of data into what a doctor can read in under a minute.

**Why it matters to the patient:** her life between visits (sleep, stress, missed walks, festivals) finally reaches the doctor, so the visit is about her, not just her last blood test.

---

## Closing line

> The doctor spends the visit on the patient, and the patient is understood from the first minute.

---

### Notes for the presenter
- Everything on screen is made-up data: the hospital, the doctor and the other patients. Only the patient flow is the real design.
- The notes assistant and the recommendations are demonstrations of what the screen can do. A clinician should review the wording and goals before real use.
- Suggested order of clicks: schedule, open the patient, hover on a visit brief box, open a domain, open a prediction, open a recommendation, click the orb.
